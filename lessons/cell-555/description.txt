A simulated robot assembly bench, running in the browser, builds a whole 555 LED blinker, played at four times speed. One arm, a turntable with a vise and pincers, an iron that feeds its own solder, a tool rack and a tray of parts. The arm brings a lead to a lead, a pincer holds it, and the turntable brings the joint to the iron. The chip goes legs up in the vise; enameled jumpers go between its legs; each resistor is bent in the rig, sheared, laid along its pin, soldered and trimmed; the capacitors are lowered from above; the LED goes onto the third resistor's upturned end; the battery snap goes on the turntable; and the cell checks the work against the netlist: seven of seven nets made, nothing damaged.

The page is driven as you would drive it: the speed menu at 4x, Play, Close up for a joint, Whole cell while the arm fetches a part, drag to turn the view, scroll to zoom, the step list following the program.
