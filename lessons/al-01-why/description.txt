Array Languages, episode 1. This may be the dawn of personal programming languages, and this is not the first one I've started creating. Most languages teach us to think one step at a time: create a variable, loop, take an element, operate, store, repeat. Array programming describes the whole computation instead. This episode compares a Rust loop with whole-array operations, traces the array family from Iverson's 1962 notation through APL\1130, APL\360, APL2, J, K and BQN, and introduces X_eTaL: an APL-inspired, statically typed array language, typed in plain ASCII and drawn as notation, with a look at what the series covers next.
