Array Languages, episode 6. Five array languages side by side: APL, J, K, BQN and X_eTaL. Running totals, reverse, right-to-left reading and a mean as a train in all five; one function written explicitly and tacitly (X_eTaL supports both, as J does); dynamic types against X_eTaL's static, inferred ones; counting from one or zero; one symbol with two meanings; how many built-ins each has; what each does best, in its own sources' words; and where X_eTaL fits. Then a first look at X_eTaL's syntax guide, the subject of episode 7. Every line in another language was run in an interpreter (GNU APL, Dyalog on TryAPL, J, Kona and ngn/k, CBQN).
